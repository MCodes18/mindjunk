# Reminiscing New York

It's been more than 10 years now, but every October, I always find myself dwelling on my memories in New York. Personally, the place feels like a dream during the fall and winter seasons. A dream I wish existed in a little pocket of its own, somewhere I could escape to occasionally. No matter how much detail I include here, it would not do full justice to the experience. Nevertheless, it's certainly worth recording.

I still remember my first time setting foot in the place - it was around late December. The city was getting ready to welcome yet another new year and still had the Christmas decorations put up. The cold was beyond numbing. One could not survive the weather without layers of clothing along with a heavy jacket, woollen gloves, muffler/beanie, warm socks and a good pair of shoes. I was utterly blown away by the setting: chilly wind, towering buildings on either side, bustling crowds from all walks of life, and yellow taxis taking up the roadspace. There were also people painted to look like the Statue of Liberty and Uncle Sam haha (I did not even realise some of them were bare-skinned, sigh), among many other things that caught my eye. Due to some financial constraints (note: NYC is not for the poor, for sure), we had to walk our way to the infamous Brooklyn Bridge. Now that I look back, I would not have it in any other way as the walking culture and the experience of the city you cultivate through it are immeasurable.

While walking, I encountered different places which my little self got excited about - the Lindt chocolate outlet (one of my absolute favourites), shops on Broadway, NY Times building, Staples (a huge store filled with stationery!? count. me. in.), warm pretzel and churro stands at every corner, NYC $1 PIZZA SLICES (delish!) and the one and only Times Square. Then there were the fresh doughnuts and scalding hot chocolate from Dunkin' Donuts. You know it will burn your tongue, but you still go for the first comforting sip anyway. I also came across crystal stores - very random, but it was the first time I saw open geodes and it was cool. I could see why the city was known as ‘The City That Never Sleeps’ - people everywhere at all hours, hustling to make a name for themselves and make ends meet. Somehow, the glimmer in their eyes to stay ‘alive’ spoke to me. Then, I came across Central Park from the Apple Store side and it was magnificent. Finally, as we reached the Brooklyn Bridge, the view was ethereal. Many people walked side by side from one end to the other. Along the way, captures were taken to solidify the memories. Small pop-up stores sold souvenirs and New Year merch (I got to try the 2015 glasses lol).

On other trips to NYC during the four years I lived in the States, I eventually got to see Rockefeller Center (the way the place glows during winter nights is a sight to behold), the High Line (an elevated park built upon an old rail line), Central Park Zoo, Ellis Island and the Statue of Liberty through cruise rides. There is so much more to write, but I will put the brakes on here for now. Moments like these make everything seem worth it. A wave of exhilaration comes over me at the thought of experiencing all of this again. Maybe next time, with my own earnings and more background knowledge, I might also catch a Broadway show, visit the Brown Building to ponder about the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory Fire and probably wear a leather jacket and comfy boots. Till then, part of me will always mentally go back there.
